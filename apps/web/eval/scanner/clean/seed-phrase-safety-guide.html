Your seed phrase: what it is and why you must never share it

By Tomasz Wilk · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

If you've just set up your first crypto wallet, you were probably shown a list of 12 or 24 ordinary English words and told to write them down. That list is your seed phrase (also called a recovery phrase or mnemonic). It is the single most important thing you own in crypto — more important than your password, your phone, or the wallet app itself.

What the words actually do

A wallet doesn't "store" your coins — they live on the blockchain. What the wallet stores is a private key that proves you're allowed to move them. Your seed phrase is a human-readable backup from which every one of your private keys can be regenerated. Type those words into any compatible wallet, anywhere in the world, and you get full control of the funds.

That's the point: if your phone falls in a lake, the words get you back in. But it also means that anyone else who sees the words gets in too — and there is no bank, no chargeback, and no "forgot my password" link to undo it.

The one rule: No legitimate person or company will ever ask for your seed phrase. Not wallet support, not an exchange, not a "validator", not an airdrop, not a friend who's "just helping". If a website, form, chat or email asks you to type it in, it is a scam. Close it.

How people actually lose their funds

The trickWhat it looks like
Fake supportYou post a question publicly and get a DM from "Wallet Support" offering to help you "sync" or "validate" your wallet.
Look-alike sitesAn ad or search result that looks exactly like your wallet's site, with a "restore wallet" form.
Airdrops & giveaways"Claim your free tokens — just verify your wallet by entering your recovery phrase."
Cloud backupsTaking a screenshot of the words that silently syncs to a photo library, which is later compromised.

A safe-storage checklist

Tick these off as you go (it's just for you — nothing here is saved):

  • I wrote my seed phrase on paper (or stamped it in metal), not in a notes app.
  • I have never photographed or screenshotted it.
  • I keep it somewhere safe from fire and water, and not labelled "crypto".
  • I considered a second copy in a separate location.
  • I understand that anyone asking for it — for any reason — is trying to steal from me.
  • I know that connecting a wallet to a site only ever requires approving a request in my wallet app, never typing words.

"But what if I really need help?"

Go directly to your wallet's official website by typing the address yourself, and use their documented help centre. Real support staff can troubleshoot without ever seeing your words. If someone insists they need them, that tells you everything.
