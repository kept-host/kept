**Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in running a secure website.

**What we store:** a scrambled form of your IP address (a hash with a key that changes every day), never the address itself. We delete these counts after 2 days.

**Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

## 9. Checking pages for abuse

**What we do:** pages on kept are public, and some people try to use them to steal logins, cheat people or spread malware. So when a page is published, replaced or restored to an earlier version, an automated check reads an extract of it. We run the check again when a page is reported, and when its traffic suddenly jumps. The rules pages must follow are in our [acceptable-use rules](/legal/acceptable-use).

**What is sent:** an extract of the page, not the file itself: the page's address, its title, headings, visible text, the labels of its form fields and buttons, text from its scripts, and the addresses its forms and scripts send data to. Nothing about the page's visitors, and nothing about its owner — no name, no email address, no account. A page can of course contain personal data its author chose to publish, such as their own name; that is then part of the extract.

**Who processes it:** the extract goes to OpenRouter, which passes it to TypeSafe's model Jev. Jev answers three questions about the page (what kind of page it is, whether it pretends to be a real company, and whether it asks visitors for passwords or payment details), and OpenRouter returns the answers to us.

**What we store:** the answers, the facts the extract was built from (such as form fields and addresses, not the page's text) and the result. We keep them as long as the page exists. If we act on a page, the record of what we did and why is kept after the page is deleted.

**What can happen:** if the check flags a page, the page is hidden while a person looks at it. The check can't remove a page and can't put one back; a person decides both. Whenever a page is hidden, removed or put back, we tell its owner by email (the account's address, or for a page published without an account, the reminder address if one was given), and show it in the studio or on the page's private link.

**Why, and the legal basis:** to keep kept and the people who visit its pages safe from fraud and malware, and to meet our duties as a host. Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in a service that isn't used for abuse.

## 10. Reports

**What we store when you report a page:**
- the page's address, what's wrong with it and your explanation;
- your name and email address, only if you give them — both are optional;
- the date and time;
- a scrambled form of your IP address (a hash with a key that changes every day), never the address itself, so we can spot repeated reports and limit how many one sender can make.

**Why:** to review the report and act on it. If you give an email address, we use it to confirm we received your report and to tell you what we decided, and for nothing else.

**How long we keep it:** we clear your name and email address 180 days after we've decided on the report. The rest of the report is kept as part of our moderation records, also after the page is deleted, so we can answer questions about our decisions and handle requests for review.

**Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in acting on reports of illegal content and abuse, and Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR, where the law requires us to process notices about illegal content.

To report a page, use the form at [kept.host/report](/report) or email abuse@kept.host.

## 12. Your rights

**You can ask us for:**
- access to your data (Art. 15 GDPR);
- correction (Art. 16);
- deletion (Art. 17);
- restriction of processing (Art. 18);
- a copy in a portable format (Art. 20).

**Consent:** you can withdraw it at any time. That doesn't affect processing that happened before you withdrew (Art. 7(3)).

**Right to object:** where we rely on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)), you can object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21).

To use any of these rights, email [[EMAIL]].

**Complaints:** you can complain to a data protection authority. The one responsible for us is the Landesbeauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit Nordrhein-Westfalen (LDI NRW): https://www.ldi.nrw.de

**Automated checks:** we do no profiling. An automated check, or a report of child sexual abuse material, can hide a page until a person has reviewed it (section 9). A person decides every removal. If your page was hidden or removed, you can ask for a review from the page in your studio, from the private link you got when you published it, or by email to abuse@kept.host.
